## Abstract

Transit surveys fold every light curve at thousands of trial periods — an exhaustive search whose cost scales poorly to the $\sim10^5$–$10^6$ stars of missions like TESS. We test, in a pre-registered single-evaluation experiment, whether detection cost can be reduced by **routing on evidence**: detecting individual transit-like events directly, inferring the period from their spacing, confirming with a calibrated folded-photometry transit likelihood-ratio held to a common false-alarm rate with the baseline, and reserving a full Transit Least Squares (TLS) search only for stars the cheap gate rejects. Using injection–recovery into real TESS light curves (preserving genuine correlated noise) on a sealed test set of 15,000 trials, we find that evidence-first routing **preserves recall** non-inferiorly to full TLS (occurrence-weighted recall difference $-0.48$ percentage points; one-sided 95% lower bound $-0.60$ pp, or $-0.82$ pp under a host-clustered bootstrap; pre-registered margin $-2$ pp). **The compute saving over the fast-path-eligible population is 27.3% (95% CI on the cost ratio [0.636, 0.826]) — short of our pre-registered 30% threshold, but with an interval straddling it, so the compute branch is inconclusive rather than falsified.** We localize the shortfall to a single replaceable component — the per-star bootstrap period false-alarm-probability (FAP) entry tax ($\rho_d\approx12\%$), charged on every routed star — which a numerical-equivalence gate proved cannot be cheapened without altering which stars pass. Recall losses concentrate on large, low-occurrence planets and arise from one mechanism (the cheap confirmer suppressing the full-search fallback on a right-period/wrong-epoch seed); they are partly offset by gains on short-period planets the periodogram misses. We release the sealed benchmark and identify the cheaper-but-equivalent period-FAP as the decisive lever for future work.

## 1. Introduction

Box-fitting (BLS; Kovács et al. 2002) and Transit Least Squares (TLS; Hippke & Heller 2019) period searches evaluate a dense grid of trial periods for every star. This exhaustive design is the workhorse of transit detection, but its cost grows with both the target count and the period-grid density, which scales unfavourably for all-sky, high-cadence missions.

An alternative is to **spend computation where the evidence is**. If a light curve contains one or more individually detectable transit-like events, their *spacing* constrains the period directly, and a single folded-photometry significance test at the implied ephemeris can confirm or reject a candidate without a full grid search. Only stars lacking such local evidence need the exhaustive search. The central question of this paper is whether such routing can cut compute **without dropping planets a full search would have found** — recall being the property a detection pipeline cannot sacrifice.

We frame this as a **non-inferiority test**, importing the logic of clinical trials: recall is the *protected* endpoint (the new procedure must be no worse than the baseline within a pre-specified margin), and compute is the *superiority* endpoint (the new procedure must be meaningfully cheaper). Both criteria, the thresholds, the injection grid, and the analysis were **pre-registered and cryptographically sealed before any test data were read**, and the test set was read exactly once. This design directly addresses the credibility problem that dogs "we saved X% compute" claims: here the result is falsifiable and was not tuned.

This is a deliberately **untrained** Phase-I test: the event detector is a simple matched-filter, not a learned model, so a pass or fail is attributable to the *routing principle* rather than to a model's capacity. We report a negative result on the compute claim with the same rigor we would a positive one.

### 2.2 Stage-0 conditioning
Each light curve is detrended per sector with a time-windowed biweight slider (wotan; Hippke et al. 2019) at a window of 2.5 d — finalized on the calibration set by a transit-preservation criterion ($\eta\ge0.90$) — with SPOC quality masking and momentum-dump handling, yielding a zero-centred residual $r(t)$. A per-star noise model ($\sigma$, CDPP, $\tau_{\rm GP}$) is recorded. No detection threshold is set at this stage.

### 2.3 Injection–recovery design
Synthetic transits are injected into **real** conditioned residuals (preserving the true correlated-noise covariance) on a grid of period $P\in\{0.5,1,2,4,8,16\}$ d and radius $R_p\in\{1,2,4,8,12\}\,R_\oplus$ (30 cells), with impact parameter varied per injection. The sealed test campaign uses **500 injections per cell** (15,000 total — the pre-registered floor) over **40 test-set host stars** (80 were drawn and conditioned; a host-assignment bug, disclosed in the erratum, used only half). Injecting into conditioned residuals — rather than the plan's raw-flux-then-recondition path — means the detrending attenuation $\eta$ measured at M2 is paid by *neither* arm: the paired $\Delta R$ is unaffected, but **absolute** completeness figures are optimistic. Recovery is defined operationally (period within tolerance and epoch within $\pm0.5\,T_{14}$), identically for both arms. Cell eligibility ($N_{\rm tr}\ge2$) uses the formula $\lfloor T_{\rm base}/P\rfloor+1$; data-driven counting shows 892 injections (5.9%, concentrated at $P=16$ d) actually presented $\le1$ observed transit — an effective-monotransit admixture shared identically by both arms.

### 2.4 The two arms (the fairness keystone)
- **Arm A (baseline):** a full-grid TLS search; a star is recovered if its signal-detection efficiency $\mathrm{SDE}\ge T$.
- **Arm B (combined / evidence-first):** the matched-filter detector extracts events; if $\ge N_{\min}$ events are found the star is *routed* and a period is inferred from event spacing; a block-bootstrap **period-FAP** gates entry; stars passing the gate go to a **folded-photometry transit likelihood-ratio (LR) confirmer** at the seeded ephemeris, and stars that fail the gate or the confirmer fall back to the **identical full TLS** as Arm A.

The fairness keystone is that both arms are held to a **common false-alarm rate**, not the same engine — a relaxation adopted in the sealed v3 amendment (below). The full-TLS fallback guarantees that a routed planet is lost only if the seed is wrong *and* the fallback is suppressed.

### 2.5 Thresholds and the v3 amendment
Thresholds were calibrated on the cleaned calibration null pool and sealed (Seal #2): event threshold $z_\star=3.4$, monotransit $z_{\rm mono}=5.3$, $N_{\min}=2$, TLS threshold $T=10.74$, period-FAP level $\alpha_{\rm FAP}=0.01$, targeted-window $\varepsilon=0.01$, an occurrence weight $w_c$ (Kunimoto & Matthews 2020 radius prior; 92.8% weight on $R_p\le2\,R_\oplus$), and a mean occurrence $\hat\pi=3.17\%$.

A pre-test methodological defect (Finding B: TLS SDE is normalized across the searched grid, so a narrow-grid SDE is not comparable to the full-grid threshold $T$) made the original "targeted-TLS" confirmation non-executable. This forced a final, pre-registered amendment (v3): the fairness keystone was relaxed from "same engine" to "common false-alarm rate," and Arm-B confirmation became the folded transit-LR ($\Lambda$, sign-aware and shape-consistent, with a physical transit template) at confirmer threshold $T_{\rm red}$ (set to the same null FAR; calibration yielded the non-binding $T_{\rm red}=0$). A separately-proposed cheapening of the period-FAP (replacing the $B{=}1000$ block bootstrap with a cheaper estimator) was admitted only under a numerical-equivalence gate, which **both** candidate estimators failed; the sealed bootstrap therefore stands. The amendment is documented in DR-002 and was sealed (Seal #2b, tag `phase1-prereg-v3`) before the test read. **No numeric threshold was changed by v3.**

### 2.6 Endpoints, pre-registration, and anti-tuning
- **E1 (recall non-inferiority):** the occurrence-weighted recall difference $\overline{\Delta R}=R_{\rm comb}-R_{\rm TLS}$; PASS if its one-sided 95% lower bound (bootstrap, $B=2000$) exceeds the $-2$ pp margin.
- **E2 (scoped compute):** the compute reduction over the fast-path-eligible population; PASS if $\ge30\%$ at non-inferior recall. (A survey-representative figure, E3, is expected near-zero by construction and is a secondary endpoint.)

The outcome mapping (E1/E2/inconclusive → conclusions) was pre-committed before the read; a stopping rule (P-1…P-9) declared v3 the terminal amendment and prohibits any post-hoc change. Integrity was verified in-run (both seals hash-checked, fail-closed) and after (`git diff` against the sealed tag empty; test accessed exactly once).

**What was *not* an endpoint (disclosure).** Precision — the false-positive rate of the combined pipeline — was deliberately **not** a pre-registered endpoint, and no result in this paper should be read as a precision claim. This follows directly from the charter's asymmetry ("a false positive is acceptable; a missed planet is not"): recall is the protected quantity (E1) and compute the superiority quantity (E2). False positives are instead controlled *by construction* rather than optimized — both arms are held to a **common calibrated false-alarm rate** (the sealed FAR ≤ 1%/star on calibration nulls; the period-FAP gate at $\alpha=0.01$ achieves an empirical 1.08%, §3.5), so any arm difference is a recall/compute difference at matched FAR, not a precision trade. We nonetheless *report*, rather than hide, the pipeline's empirical false-positive behavior: the null FAR calibration (§3.5, Figure F6) and the reality-check **eclipsing-binary leakage** — ~25% of known EBs survive the layered vetting (§3.6, Table T6) — together stand as the precision proxy. A precision/purity endpoint on a labeled astrophysical-FP set (beyond the small EB sample here) is left to future work; it was outside this experiment's pre-registered scope, and the EB leakage is stated plainly as the known limitation.

## 3. Results

The single sealed-test evaluation (15,000 injections; read once) returns the headline of Table T2.

### 3.1 E1 — recall is preserved (PASS, robust across interval methods)
The occurrence-weighted recall difference is $\overline{\Delta R}=-0.48$ pp. Because all 15,000 injections share only 40 host noise realizations, we report the one-sided 95% lower bound under three constructions: the injection-level paired bootstrap ($-0.60$ pp), a **host-cluster bootstrap** that resamples the 40 hosts ($-0.82$ pp), and the pre-registered Wilson-style weighted combination ($-0.60$ pp). All clear the $-2$ pp margin decisively: **routing does not sacrifice recall.** The pass is not an artifact of the sealed weight's **log-uniform period dimension**: re-weighting periods by the Kunimoto & Matthews (2020) occurrence distribution (their Eqn 22/25 broken power law per radius bin; radius weights held fixed) leaves E1 non-inferior — in fact *stronger*, $\overline{\Delta R}=-0.16$ pp (worst-case lower bound $-0.22$ pp across the three interval methods and both handlings of the below-support $P=0.5$ d node), because real occurrence concentrates at long periods where the two arms are at near-parity and down-weights the short/mid periods that carry the combined arm's losses (RES-2 sensitivity; `data/manifests/m4/wave1/res2_km_period_sensitivity.md`). The heavy long-period weighting does lean on the $P=16$ d cells that carry the effective-monotransit caveat of §2.3/§4(iii). On the occurrence-weighted scale (Table T2) recall is 0.218 (full) vs 0.213 (combined); on the unweighted injected grid it is 0.509 vs 0.488 (absolute figures optimistic per §2.3's residual-injection note). The weighted figures are low in absolute terms because the Kunimoto & Matthews (2020) weight concentrates on small ($R_p\le2$) planets, which are intrinsically hard; E1 concerns the *difference*, which is non-inferior.

Two structural facts qualify what the pass demonstrates: 30.3% of the total occurrence weight lies in cells where **both** arms have zero recall (so $\Delta R_c\equiv0$ identically), and 78.9% of injections took the full-TLS fallback, where the combined arm's outcome is by construction Arm A's. Non-inferiority is therefore in substantial part a property of the *fallback architecture plus the occurrence weighting*; the cells where the fast path genuinely operates ($R_p=4$–$12$) show per-cell losses up to $-25.6$ pp at $<8\%$ combined weight. This is disclosed rather than hidden: it is precisely the recall-safety-by-construction the architecture was designed to deliver, but it bounds how much the pass says about the cheap path in isolation.

The completeness maps (Figure F3) and Table T3 localize the structure. At $R_p=2\,R_\oplus$ — the weight-dominant class — the arms are at near-parity ($-0.37$ pp). The largest per-cell losses are at intermediate periods and larger radii ($R_p=4$: $-5.1$ pp; $R_p=8/12$: $-2.1/-2.7$ pp), which carry little occurrence weight. At $R_p=1$ both arms recover essentially nothing (noise-limited — too shallow to detect at any period in this baseline). Notably, at the shortest period ($P=0.5$ d) the combined arm **outperforms** full TLS by $+19$ pp (Table T3, by period; the blue column in F3). A dedicated control (Supplement S-edge; Figure S1; erratum §6) shows the mechanism is *not* detection power and *not* a TLS grid-edge artifact (extending the grid below $P_{\min}$ changes nothing, per-row bit-identically): full TLS at $P=0.5$ d finds the right period at high SDE but fails the recovery predicate on **epoch** — its fitted $T_0$ falls outside $\pm0.5\,T_{14}$ in $\sim$95% of its failures there (36 of 38) — while the combined arm's detector-seeded epoch survives the same predicate.

### 3.2 Recall-loss mechanism (one pathway)
Against full TLS the outcomes are: both recover 6,761; neither 6,807; **combined-only (gain) 563; TLS-only (loss) 869.** All 869 losses share a single pathway: the cheap confirmer confirmed at the seeded ephemeris and therefore **suppressed the full-TLS fallback**, but the seed then failed the recovery predicate. Of these, 80% are *right-period/wrong-epoch* — the detector's event epoch is less precise than TLS's fitted $T_0$ — and 20% are wrong-period. They concentrate on large, low-occurrence planets (so the weighted $\overline{\Delta R}$ stays small). The 563 gains, almost all at $P=0.5$ d, partly offset them — and the edge control (erratum §6) shows they are the *mirror image* of the loss pathway: TLS failing the epoch predicate where the seeded ephemeris passes it. Both the gains and 80% of the losses are epoch-predicate phenomena, not detection-power differences. This mechanism was anticipated by the calibration dress rehearsal and is a confirmer/fallback interaction (the non-binding $T_{\rm red}=0$ provides no recall floor), not a failure of period recovery. An epoch-tolerance sensitivity confirms the predicate-choice character: loosening the combined arm's epoch window from $\pm0.5$ to $\pm0.75/\pm1.0\,T_{14}$ reclassifies 260/402 of the 693 right-period losses as recoveries, lifting the (combined-side, occurrence-weighted) $\overline{\Delta R}$ from $-0.48$ pp to $-0.17/-0.00$ pp (RES-3; `data/manifests/m4/wave1/res3_epoch_tolerance_sensitivity.md`). These are upper bounds — a symmetric sweep (needing stored TLS epochs) would also recover TLS's own wrong-epoch cases, the $P=0.5$ d gains — but they establish that the loss channel is epoch-precision, not detection power, and motivate an epoch-refit confirmer (future work).

### 3.3 E2 — compute saving is inconclusive (short of 30%, interval straddles it)
Under the owner-frozen timing rule (stratified ≥10 fast-path-eligible LCs per occupied cell, cap 300, ≥5 warm-cache repeats, single-thread CPU-core-seconds — the procedure the sealed run under-delivered with a 12-star, one-repeat measurement; DR-003), the combined arm costs **0.727× the full search — a 27.3% reduction**, over 300 injections across 39 hosts (authoritative values and per-stage costs: erratum §5; Table T7/Figure F8 predate the re-measurement and are regenerated at revision). The host-clustered bootstrap 95% CI on the ratio is **[0.636, 0.826]**: it straddles the pre-registered 0.70 boundary (P(ratio ≤ 0.70) = 0.27), so by VAL §5's decision rule (hi ≤ 0.70 PASS · lo > 0.70 FAIL · else INCONCLUSIVE) the compute branch is **INCONCLUSIVE** — the saving is real (~27%) but neither reaches 30% nor is decisively below it. The bottleneck is the routing **entry tax** $\rho_d\approx11.6\%$: the detector plus the $B=1000$ block-bootstrap period-FAP are charged on every routed star, including the majority that fail the FAP gate and fall back to the full search (of routed stars, only $f_p=23.7\%$ confirm on the cheap path). F8 makes this concrete: confirmed-cheap stars save dramatically (~90 s → ~10 s), but fallback stars pay the entry tax *on top of* the full search, so the per-star saving is diluted. The break-even occurrence under the theory's definition ($\pi^\star=\rho_d/f_p(1-\rho)$ with $f_p$ the confirmed-cheap fraction) is $\pi^\star\approx0.489$ — the originally reported 0.236 mistakenly used a search-cost fraction for $f_p$ (erratum §2.6/§5.2). Either way $\pi^\star$ is ~16× TESS-realistic occurrence ($\pi\approx0.03$), reinforcing the pre-registered conclusion that survey-scale saving requires a clean-skip tier.

### 3.4 Verdict
By the pre-committed mapping (VAL §5/§7a): **E1 PASS · E2 INCONCLUSIVE.** Recall non-inferiority is established (robustly); the scoped compute hypothesis is neither confirmed nor falsified at decision-grade precision. A decisive execution of a pre-registered experiment whose compute branch it could not resolve — reported with the same rigor as a positive result. (This supersedes the sealed run's recorded "E2 FAIL → H1 FALSIFIED, compute branch," which the audit traced to a procedural deviation, not a protocol outcome; DR-003.)

### 3.5 Parameter recovery and calibration
Among the 11,036 routed injections with a period seed (Table T5; Figure F5), the inferred period matches truth within tolerance for 45.9%, with a tight median fractional error of 0.0022 for matched cases; 27.1% of matches fall on a 2×/½/3× harmonic of the fundamental rather than the fundamental itself. The seeded epoch lies within the recovery window ($\le0.5\,T_{14}$) for 54.2%, with a median offset of $0.40\,T_{14}$ — the detector-epoch imprecision (relative to TLS's fitted $T_0$) that drives the right-period/wrong-epoch loss pathway of §3.2. The period-FAP is well-calibrated: on the cleaned null pool the empirical false-alarm rate at the sealed $\alpha=0.01$ is **1.08%** (Figure F6, Table T4), reproducing the sealed M3 calibration. F6 also quantifies the value of the null-pool cleaning — before removing EB/variable contaminants (which carry real periodic signals) the raw FAR is inflated to 2.07%. The confirmer's fitted **depth is biased low by ~20%** (median fractional error −0.201; Table T5; Figure F5b) and the detector's **seed duration is ~31% short** (−0.312; Figure F5c, reflecting the coarse duration grid) — biases that contribute to the cheap path's reduced parameter precision and the wrong-epoch loss pathway (§3.2).

### 3.6 Reality check on real objects (test-blind; Table T6)
Run on **real** confirmed planets and known eclipsing binaries (calibration-split only; the test split is never touched), the pipeline recovers **86.7% of 30 confirmed/known TOIs**, with **Arm B equal to Arm A** — the evidence-first router drops no real confirmed planet relative to full TLS, corroborating the injection-based E1 result on real planets. On 16 known eclipsing binaries, the layered gate rejects **12/16** (8 stopped by the period-FAP gate, 4 by the confirmer's odd-even / secondary-eclipse vetting); **4 (~25%) slip through as false positives** — a genuine limitation, as EBs with planet-like primary eclipses can survive the vetting. *(Samples are small; these are point estimates with wide uncertainty.)*

### 3.7 What the calibrated gate buys (ablation; Table T8)
Ablating the gate on calibration shows a clean division of labor: removing the **bootstrap period-FAP gate** raises the null false-positive rate from 0.0% to **12.3%** — it is the FAP gate that controls false alarms; removing the **shape/sign vetting** raises cheap-path recall (0.133 → 0.189) at no clean-null FP cost, so its value is *astrophysical* false-positive rejection (it caught 4/8 EBs in §3.6) rather than noise rejection. This quantifies the contribution of calibrated photometric gating and directly rebuts the prior version's "false positives are noise artifacts" failure mode.

## 4. Discussion

**What holds.** The recall-safety of evidence-first routing — its hardest and most important claim — is *supported* on a sealed test set: a cheap detector plus a calibrated transit-LR confirmer plus a full-search fallback recovers planets non-inferiorly to exhaustive TLS at a common false-alarm rate. The architecture does what it was designed to do for recall, and the reality check corroborates this on **real** confirmed planets (Arm B recovers the same 86.7% as full TLS, §3.6). The ablation (§3.7) shows the calibrated period-FAP gate is what controls false alarms (0→12.3% if removed), defending the photometric-gating design against the prior version's "FP = noise" failure.

**What is undecided, and why it is informative.** The compute claim is *not resolved* in this realization — the 27.3% saving falls short of 30% but its interval straddles the threshold — and whatever the shortfall, it is *localized to one replaceable component*: the bootstrap period-FAP entry tax. We did not merely observe a shortfall — we proved (via the equivalence gate) that this particular estimator cannot be cheapened without changing which stars pass, and we measured exactly how the tax dilutes the saving (Figure F8). The principle is neither refuted nor confirmed on compute; a specific, identified configuration is the binding constraint.

**Scope and caveats.** (i) The 27.3% (95% CI on the cost ratio [0.636, 0.826]) is the *scoped* reduction over fast-path-eligible stars, measured under the frozen §6 timing rule on 300 injections across 39 hosts (superseding the sealed run's 12-star, one-repeat 24.4% estimate; DR-003); the survey-representative figure is expected near-zero by construction and is not claimed. (ii) Relaxing the fairness keystone to common-FAR (v3) means an arm difference also reflects the intrinsic power gap between the LR confirmer and TLS, not routing alone; the full-TLS fallback mitigates this and the caveat is disclosed. (iii) **The eligibility axis is formula-level:** all 15,000 injections had $\ge2$ transits by the pre-registered formula $\lfloor T_{\rm base}/P\rfloor+1$, but data-driven counting shows 892 (5.9%; 34% of the $P=16$ d injections) presented $\le1$ observed transit in the gappy baselines — an effective-monotransit admixture faced identically by both arms. A *deliberate* monotransit campaign (the known evidence-first advantage region, since full TLS cannot fold single events) remains unexercised and deferred. (iv) **The confirmer's astrophysical vetting is partial:** ~25% of known eclipsing binaries slip through as false positives (§3.6), and the cheap-path depth/duration estimates are biased (depth ~20% low, seed duration ~31% short; §3.5) — both point to a stronger confirmer (better odd-even/secondary discrimination; a depth/duration refit) as concrete future work. (v) **At the sealed operating point the binding false-alarm control is the period-FAP gate, not the photometric confirmer:** $T_{\rm red}$ calibrated to zero (non-binding), so the confirmer contributes only its sign and shape vetoes, and the ablation (§3.7) confirms the FAP gate carries the false-alarm budget. The project's design principle — *photometric significance, not timing coincidence, as the arbiter* — is realized here only through those vetoes; a future operating point with a binding $\Lambda$ threshold would realize it fully.

**Future work (separately pre-registered).** The decisive lever is a **cheaper-but-provably-equivalent period-FAP** — the direction the equivalence gate closed for the two candidates tried, but which a better-constructed estimator could pass. Other directions (multi-harmonic testing; a recall-protective confirmer floor to remove the right-period/wrong-epoch losses; a clean-skip tier) are deferred to new experiments. None is a continuation of this one: v3 is the terminal amendment.

## 5. Conclusion

Evidence-first routing on TESS preserves recall but, in its sealed realization, does not reach a 30% compute saving at decision-grade precision (27.3%, 95% CI [0.636, 0.826] straddling the threshold) — an inconclusive compute branch, traced to the per-star bootstrap period-FAP entry tax. The recall principle survives; the compute claim awaits a cheaper, equivalent gate to be settled either way. We release the sealed benchmark, thresholds, and evaluation code to support replication and the identified next step.

## Supplement

### S-edge. The P = 0.5 d "gain region" is an epoch-predicate phenomenon (control)

**Motivation.** 561 of the combined arm's 563 gains over full TLS (§3.1–§3.2) sit at the shortest grid period, $P=0.5$ d — exactly the sealed TLS grid's `period_min`. A gain concentrated at a grid boundary invites the reading that it is a search-configuration artifact. We test this directly with a calibration-only control (`edge_control.py`; the sealed TEST split is never touched).

**Design.** For $R_p=8\,R_\oplus$ injections into 59 cleaned calibration null hosts (60 paired injections per condition, sealed $T=10.74$): **A** — $P=0.5$ d on the sealed grid ($P_{\min}=0.5$); **B** — the identical injections with the grid extended to $P_{\min}=0.3$; **C** — $P=0.62$ d (off-node) on the sealed grid.

**Result (Table S-edge; Figure S1).**

| Condition | recall | period recovered | SDE ≥ T | median SDE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A — P = 0.5 d, sealed grid | 0.367 | 0.983 | 0.967 | 22.4 |
| B — P = 0.5 d, grid → $P_{\min}=0.3$ | 0.367 | 0.983 | 0.967 | 22.4 |
| C — P = 0.62 d, off-node | 0.983 | 0.983 | 0.983 | 25.7 |

Conditions A and B are **bit-identical per row**: extending the grid below the boundary changes nothing, so the shortfall at $P=0.5$ d is **not** a grid-edge artifact. Instead, full TLS at $P=0.5$ d finds the right period at high SDE (well above $T$; Figure S1a) but fails the recovery predicate on **epoch** — **36 of the 38** A-condition failures are epoch-only (fitted $T_0$ outside $\pm0.5\,T_{14}$; Figure S1b), versus 0 of 1 at the off-node $P=0.62$ d. The $P=0.5$ d "gain region" is thus the *mirror image* of the dominant loss channel (§3.2): a $T_0$-precision failure of TLS at ultra-short periods that the combined arm's detector-seeded epoch happens to survive. Both the gains and ~80% of the losses are epoch-predicate phenomena, not detection-power differences. *(Per-injection $T_0$ errors were not persisted by the control run; a $T_0$-error histogram would require re-running it to store $T_0$.)* Artifacts: `data/manifests/m4/e2_retiming/edge_control.{csv,json}`; figure `research/m4_evaluation/figures/S1_edge_control.png`.
